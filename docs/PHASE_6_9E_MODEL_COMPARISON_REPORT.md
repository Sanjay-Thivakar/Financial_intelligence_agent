## 2. Experimental Design & Invariant Controls

To prevent data snooping, artificial metric optimization, and leakage, all candidate models adhered to identical experimental controls:
1. **Identical Walk-Forward Folds**: All models were fitted and evaluated on the exact same 4 expanding walk-forward folds generated by [`src/models/splitter.py`](file:///c:/Users/sanja/OneDrive/Documents/Sanjay/Colllege_Projects/Stock_prediction_pinnacle_project/project/src/models/splitter.py) with 5-session purge gaps.

3. **Training-Only Preprocessing**: Imputation (`SimpleImputer(strategy="median")`) and scaling (`StandardScaler()`) were fitted strictly on each outer training fold and applied out-of-sample via `transform()`. The non-linear tree model (`HistGradientBoostingRegressor`) utilized native missingness routing without synthetic imputation.
4. **Untouched Final Holdout**: Zero observations from the post-June 3, 2020 period were used in training, validation, or hyperparameter selection.

## 4. Deep Algorithmic and Econometric Diagnosis

### 4.1 Why Cleaned Stationary Features (M2) Do Not Improve RMSE
- Model M2 eliminated the raw dollar price SMAs (`market_sma_20d`, `market_sma_50d`), dropped exact duplicate momentum features, and removed zero-variance constants, replacing them with stationary distance-to-SMA ratios.
- **Empirical Impact**: RMSE remained essentially flat (`0.0730` vs `0.0731`), while directional hit rate declined slightly from $54.0\%$ to $52.2\%$.
- **Financial Diagnosis**: While cleaning nominal price features restored proper econometric stationarity, the underlying information content of 5-day price momentum and volatility was already captured by `market_return_5d` and `market_volatility_5d`. The additional fundamental ratios (quarterly lag) and event counts (76% zeros) did not inject sufficient incremental signal to reduce forward return variance.

### 4.2 Why Non-Linear Gradient Boosted Trees (M3) Failed to Outperform Ridge
A common hypothesis in machine learning is that fundamental ratios require non-linear decision trees to model complex conditional relationships (e.g., high ROA is only valuable if Debt/Equity is low).
- We trained `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` (max depth 3, $L_2$ regularization = 1.0, 50 trees).
- **Result**: HistGBR achieved an aggregate RMSE of `0.0731`, identical to the baseline, but suffered degraded directional hit rate ($51.5\%$) and negative rank IC ($-0.022$).
- **Root Cause**:
  1. **Noise-to-Signal Ratio**: In daily financial asset pricing, the signal-to-noise ratio of forward returns is typically below 5%. Non-linear trees with multiple split thresholds tend to partition idiosyncratic return noise, resulting in poor out-of-sample generalization.
  2. **Inability to Extrapolate Trend**: Decision trees are step-function estimators that cannot extrapolate outside the training feature range. During the unprecedented March 2020 COVID crash, HistGBR predictions bounded at the historical training bounds, failing to track extreme market tails.
  3. **Linear Regularization Superiority**: Linear Ridge regression, by applying uniform $L_2$ shrinkage toward zero, acts as an optimal shrinkage filter in high-noise regimes.

### 4.3 Why Market-Neutral Excess Return Modeling (M4) Yielded Inconclusive IC
- Model M4 decomposed the target by subtracting the daily cross-sectional market average return ($\bar{y}_{mkt}$) during training, forcing the model to focus exclusively on cross-sectional relative performance.
- When evaluated out-of-sample, pure cross-sectional excess rank IC achieved a mean daily IC of **$-0.0314$ ($t = -1.10, p = 0.274$)**.
- **Financial Diagnosis**:
  1. Decoupling macro beta successfully isolated idiosyncratic stock return variation.
  2. However, trailing accounting facts (reported 20–45 days after quarter-end on 90-day intervals) and raw unsigned 8-K counts have virtually zero correlation with 5-day cross-sectional forward drift.
  3. Standard linear fundamental factors in financial literature (Fama-French, AQR) require holding horizons of **1 month to 12 months** to realize statistical significance. Over a 5-day trading window, market microstructure and noise overwhelm trailing accounting ratios.

## 5. Metric Interpretation and Econometric Validity

### 5.1 Why Lower RMSE Does Not Imply Higher Hit Rate
- RMSE is a quadratic distance metric:
  $$\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}$$
  It heavily penalizes large forecast errors (e.g., predicting $+1\%$ when the stock drops $-15\%$).
- Directional hit rate is a binary classification metric:
  $$\text{Hit Rate} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}(\text{sign}(y_i) == \text{sign}(\hat{y}_i))$$
- A naive baseline that always predicts $+0.005$ (like `Reference 1 Train Median`) makes small, persistent errors on negative days, achieving a high hit rate ($54.7\%$) in bull markets solely by riding positive drift. However, when the market crashes (Fold 2), its squared errors explode, yielding inferior RMSE ($0.1137$).
- A well-calibrated shrinkage model (like Ridge) shrinks predictions toward zero and predicts negative returns when short-term momentum breaks down. It achieves lower overall squared error (RMSE $0.0728$), but its binary hit rate is lower because it takes active two-sided directional bets.

### 5.2 Appropriateness and Limitations of Newey-West HAC Inference
1. **Serial Autocorrelation**: Because the target is a 5-day forward return ($H=5$), consecutive daily forecasts overlap by 4 sessions, creating moving-average $MA(4)$ serial dependence in the daily $IC_t$ series.
2. **Bartlett Kernel Validity**: Applying Newey-West HAC with lag truncation $L = H - 1 = 4$ is the textbook econometric procedure to adjust standard errors for overlapping forecast horizons.
3. **Sample Size Limitations**: Across the 180 out-of-sample trading dates ($T=180$), HAC standard errors are $\approx 0.032 - 0.035$. With a standard error of $0.035$, an Information Coefficient would need to exceed $|IC| > 0.070$ to achieve $2\sigma$ statistical significance ($p < 0.05$). The observed daily ICs (ranging from $-0.016$ to $-0.037$) are well within the random noise band.

### 5.3 Trading Profitability Disclaimer
Neither a $54.0\%$ directional hit rate nor an RMSE of $0.0728$ indicates a profitable live trading strategy. Net profitability requires positive alpha that survives bid-ask spreads, transaction commissions, market impact, borrow fees for short positions, and execution slippage. In the absence of positive, statistically significant daily IC, simulated backtests would inevitably show negative risk-adjusted returns after friction.
